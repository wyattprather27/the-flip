The original story led with a single plant's layoff announcement — 400 jobs cut at a regional
auto-parts supplier — and framed it as evidence of a manufacturing sector in retreat. The layoff
happened. But it's one data point being asked to carry a trend it doesn't support.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' most recent employment report shows manufacturing payrolls grew
for a third consecutive month, adding roughly 22,000 jobs nationally over that span. Layoffs at
individual plants are common even in expanding sectors; they show up in the same monthly data as
net job gains elsewhere. Citing one without the other isn't false, but it isn't the full picture.

None of this means manufacturing is booming — growth has been modest, and regional divergence is
real: the plant in question is in a sub-sector (auto parts) that has been softer than the
sector-wide average. A story about that specific weakness would be accurate. A story that uses it
to imply sector-wide contraction isn't.
